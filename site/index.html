NoChords

A chart that plays itself.
Chords that fade as you learn them.

Type a song in once. Press play and the chart scrolls line by line at your tempo, counted in, so your hands stay on the instrument. Every playthrough hides a few more chords — until you can follow the lyrics alone.

Install it on a phone

iPhone or iPad
Open songs.nochords.app in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
Android
Open it in Chrome, tap the ⋮ menu, then Install app or Add to Home screen.

It then opens full screen from its own icon, and keeps working offline.

It plays itself

Set the tempo, get counted in, and the chart moves with the song: the line being sung is marked, the beat is on screen, and the lines you have passed step back. Tap a line to jump. The screen stays on while it plays.

It hides the chords

Level one shows the verse and chorus whole and thins their repeats. Level two hides half. Level three hides most of it. Tap a line to peek — it never costs you progress.

It reads in degrees

One tap turns the chart into roman numerals: Am C D F becomes i III IV VI. Degrees describe the song rather than the key, so they stay put when you transpose.

The beat strip shows the meter, the beat sounding, and where you are in the line. A blurred chord is one you are learning to remember — tap the line to see it.

How it works

Every playthrough, a little less to lean on

Each playthrough is a level. The first shows the verse and chorus whole and only starts thinning their repeats; the second hides half of every section; the third hides most of it, opening chords included. Set a level yourself, or let it climb on its own.

Lost a chord? Tap the line and it comes back for a few seconds — a glance at the answer, not a change of state.

Notation

Write it the way you would scribble it

One line per lyric line, with chords in brackets where they fall in the words.

You typeThere [Am]is a [C]house in New [D]Orleans, [F]
You play
There Amis a Chouse in New DOrleans, F
  • |4|at the end of a line — it lasts four bars instead of the song's usual
  • {3/4}at the start of a line — the meter changes from here on
  • ♩ = 90a tempo is a note and a number, because a bare BPM does not say what it counts

Transpose with a tap; the chords follow and the degrees stay put.
